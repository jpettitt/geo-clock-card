About GeoClock

GeoClock is a live map of day and night on Earth — the real solar terminator sweeping across NASA satellite imagery, with local clocks for the places you care about. It's free, open source, and runs everywhere from a browser tab to your desktop wallpaper.

The story

GeoClock started as a Home Assistant dashboard card: a homage to the classic mechanical world clocks that scroll a lit map past a fixed hour scale (Geochron® is the famous one — GeoClock has no affiliation with it). Once the card could render anywhere, the other surfaces followed almost for free: this website, a Chrome new-tab page, a macOS live wallpaper, and a screensaver — all driven by the same rendering code.

How it works

  • Day and night sides come from NASA's Blue Marble and Black Marble imagery (public domain), blended along the real terminator computed from solar geometry — including proper twilight feathering.
  • Time zones come from timezone-boundary-builder (ODbL), so the hover popups and hour bands are DST-aware.
  • Everything renders in your browser (or the app's local web view). There is no backend: no accounts, no tracking, and your location never leaves your device — details in the privacy policy.

Where it runs

  • geoclock.world — the live map, right here, with markers and share links.
  • Home Assistant card — the original: a Lovelace custom card, installable via HACS.
  • GeoClock New Tab — every new Chrome tab is the map, fully offline.
  • GeoClock Wallpaper for Mac — your desktop is the map, with ticking clocks.
  • GeoClock screensaver — the companion saver, fed by the Mac app.

Open source

All of it is GPL-3.0-or-later, built by John Pettitt. The card, website, and extension live in geo-clock-card; the Mac app and screensaver in geoclock-wallpaper-mac. Bug reports and ideas are welcome in the GitHub issues.
